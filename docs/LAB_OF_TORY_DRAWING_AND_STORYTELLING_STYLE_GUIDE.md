# Lab of Tory: Drawing and Storytelling Style Guide

Research date: 2026-11-06. Status: research phase. No renderer, pipeline or existing creative guide was changed.

**How to read this guide**

- **[M] Measured:** computed from the sampled videos.
- **[O] Observed:** seen in frames by the researcher, with a video and time where possible.
- **[P] Proposed:** a Lab of Tory rule. It is a design decision, not a fact about the channels, and none has been tested on a Lab render.

## The five questions, answered

| Question | Proposed answer | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **When should Tory appear?** | In the opening beat, then whenever the point is about a person: choosing, reacting, doubting. Tory leaves the focus when the idea needs the whole frame | Casually Explained cuts between its large narrator figure and full-frame diagrams [O] |
| **What should Tory do while the narrator speaks?** | Hold a simple pose and do one purposeful action per sentence at most: point, carry, look, flinch. Idle otherwise | Sam O'Nella and Casually Explained keep 85–96% of frames nearly static [M] |
| **When should a new drawing appear?** | On the word that names a new thing. A new whole scene appears only when the place, time or argument changes | Tarrare and Levels of Wealth examples, section 17 [O] |
| **When should the drawing stay still?** | During explanation of something already shown, during joke setup, and for the held turn | Casually Explained's pyramid stays unchanged for about 6 seconds while the narrator explains it [M+O] |
| **When should Tory react?** | Just after the fact lands, not during it. Reaction first, then the next idea | Proposed from comedy structure. Exact reaction delay was **not measured** at the sampled frame rate |
| **How should drawings change as the story progresses?** | Grow, then clear. Add to the board within an idea, change the whole board between ideas, and bring an early object back at the end | CGP Grey's timeline grows object by object; After Skool's board extends sideways [O] |

## Key research findings

1. **Stillness is normal.** [M] The share of nearly still frames ranged from 15% (Kurzgesagt) to 96% (Casually Explained). The two simplest comedy channels were among the stillest, so simple drawings do not need constant motion.
2. **There is no universal pace.** [M] Median time between major visual changes ran from 1.0 s (TheOdd1sOut) to 16 s (Sprouts). The median across the 12 channels was about 4.4 s, close to The Lab's current 3.6–4 s.
3. **Comedy channels change pictures on meaning, not on a clock.** [O] In hand-checked samples, new objects matched the words naming them. The bot should key drawings to nouns and events, not fixed intervals.
4. **Word-level sync could not be proven statistically.** [M] Captions contain so many words that chance alone matched almost as often as the real events. Hand-checked examples support sync, but the batch numbers do not. See section 17.

## 1. Creative identity [P]

The Lab of Tory is a **working blueprint notebook**: Tory, an inventive lab presenter, explains how something works by building the explanation on a blue grid, live.

- **Keep:** Tory's existing design and colors, the blue blueprint board, the grid, hand-lettered Caveat labels, the pale-blue and amber accents, and the pencil tip that shows a drawing being made.
- **Be different:** no hand filming, no flat stick-figure cast, no glossy motion-graphics world and no photo collage. The channels supply principles, never looks.
- **Why it belongs:** a blueprint is naturally built piece by piece, so progressive drawing has a reason here.

## 2. Drawing philosophy [P]

1. A drawing is a sentence. If removing it loses no meaning, cut it.
2. Draw the idea, not the topic. "Pressure builds" needs a rising gauge, not another volcano.
3. Add one new thing at a time.
4. Show comparisons side by side. Levels of Wealth lays out the family-size comparison as two rows [O].
5. Use a keyword card only when no honest picture exists. Never use an unrelated picture.

## 3. Character drawing rules [P]

- Use only the existing rig. Do not redraw or restyle Tory.
- Keep Tory's on-screen scale fixed, except for an intended close-up.
- Tory stays on the ground line, in the lower third of the portrait frame. Drawings stay above Tory.
- Supporting people are simple, with one identifying feature, so that Tory remains the most detailed character. CGP Grey uses one prop to identify a role, and OverSimplified uses a simple recurring figure [O].
- Do not give a character more detail than the joke or idea needs.

## 4. Drawing appearance and disappearance

**Observed**

- Sprouts and After Skool draw progressively. Sprouts works in line first and adds color later. After Skool adds color after line art [O].
- Sam O'Nella, Casually Explained and MinuteEarth mostly place objects in view without a visible draw-on [O].
- CGP Grey adds objects to an existing diagram, such as a timeline filling up across several frames [O].
- Removal is usually a whole-scene change rather than an object fading out [O].

**Proposed**

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Main drawing | Draw on with the pencil tip |
| Added detail | Draw on in 0.3–0.6 s |
| Label | Starts after its object is recognizable |
| Removal within an idea | Keep it, unless it distracts |
| Removal between ideas | Clear the board in one quick move |
| First frame | Must already show something, so a viewer never starts on an empty board |

**Proposed Lab beat types**

| Beat | Length | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Stab | 0.8–1.5 s | Punchline, sudden event |
| Build | 1.5–3 s | One addition to the board |
| Explain | 3–5 s | Default beat |
| Hold | 6–10 s | Turning point or diagram being explained |

In a 60-second video, aim for 12–18 whole-scene changes or fewer, 15–25 added elements and at least one hold.

## 6. Animation and movement

**Observed:** Sam O'Nella's figures stay stiff while a single arm raises for a gesture. Casually Explained animates tiny details such as a heart monitor. TheOdd1sOut changes poses and expressions constantly. OverSimplified uses bursts of fast action inside otherwise simple scenes, such as the figure slashing the wall map.

**Proposed**

- Idle Tory always: breathing, blinks and lip sync from the existing rig.
- One major gesture per sentence at most.
- Tie gestures to verbs and object words: "this" points, "builds" lifts, "breaks" flinches.
- Between gestures, return to a neutral pose.
- Reuse drawings by changing state. Add a crack, fill a gauge, or change a label. Do not just repeat the same picture.

## 7. Facial expressions and reactions

**Observed:** Simple faces carry jokes through the eyes and mouth. MinuteEarth's wizard shows confusion with one question mark. CGP Grey's small emotive figures use one altered face.

**Proposed vocabulary**

| Expression | Trigger |
|---|---|
| Curious | A question |
| Delighted | The discovery |
| Worried | Risk or rising tension |
| Flat stare | Absurd fact |
| Shock | A sudden event |
| Satisfied | The payoff |

Use one expression per beat. For a reaction, freeze for a brief moment, change the face, hold, then continue. Exact timing needs testing because the research sampled comedy at 1 frame per second.

## 8. Scene composition

**Observed:** Casually Explained and Sam O'Nella use mostly white backgrounds and add a detailed background only when place matters, like the house and field in Tarrare. MinuteEarth keeps most scenes on near-white with a few key objects. Sprouts builds denser scenes, then clears them.

**Proposed**

- One focal object at a time. No more than 3–5 meaningful objects on the board.
- Keep the blueprint grid as the default background. Add location detail only when it matters.
- Keep captions, drawings and Tory in their existing zones.
- Direct attention with the pencil, arrows, Tory's gaze, or a change in accent color.
- Leave empty space, because it signals what comes next and keeps the board readable.

## 9. Visual storytelling

| Technique | Observation | Lab of Tory use |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Kurzgesagt opens in action. Sprouts sets out four categories immediately [O] | Open with the problem already drawn |
| Character as idea | CGP Grey's staff-carrying figure stands for a role [O] | One symbol for each abstract force |
| Visual joke | Tarrare's belly flap and stink lines illustrate the fact literally [O] | Draw the fact literally, then let Tory react |
| Suspense | Kurzgesagt darkens the scene on bad news [O] | Dim the blueprint at the turn. Use sparingly |
| Recurring character | TheOdd1sOut's narrator persists across every scene [O] | Tory is the constant |
| Callback | MinuteEarth returns to its queen bee through the sequence [O] | Bring the first object back in the ending |
| Topic transition | Casually Explained cuts on topic phrases such as "moving beyond the middle class" [O] | Change the scene on the transition phrase |
| Final image | Not reliably sampled: only opening sections were studied | Show the first drawing changed by what we learned [P] |

## 10. Scriptwriting

**Observed openings:** direct address (Sam O'Nella), a strange fact (MinuteEarth: a bee's fatal mating), a character detail with a joke (OverSimplified on Churchill), a quote with a claim (After Skool), a date and name (TED-Ed) and a scene in progress (Kurzgesagt).

**Proposed**

- Hook in the first sentence, ideally under 12 words.
- Sponsors never come before the hook.
- Write short sentences. Write the words so each sentence can trigger one visual idea.
- Mark each line as `SHOW` (a new visual), `HOLD` (same visual) or `TURN` (the key moment).
- Prefer one concrete analogy per video.
- End on a consequence or a new way to see something.

Sentence length was not measured, because auto-captions have no reliable punctuation.

## 11. Comedy and emotional timing [P]

Based on observation of setups and punchlines, not measured reaction times.

1. **Setup:** picture holds still, narration states the normal thing.
2. **Punchline:** the absurd word and the picture change land together.
3. **Reaction:** Tory's expression changes after the word.
4. **Release:** hold briefly, then move on without explaining the joke.

No more than one joke per major idea. Do not joke during serious topics such as illness, death or tragedy. Those scenes should get slower pacing, restrained expressions and no gags.

## 12. Narration synchronization

**Which words trigger change**

| Word type | Example | Visual response |
|---|---|---|
| New noun | "the magma" | Draw the object |
| Change verb | "explodes" | Change state |
| Transition | "but", "meanwhile", "years later" | Clear or shift the scene |
| Number | "one in five" | Show the quantity |
| Name | "Mussolini" | Add a figure and label, as OverSimplified does at 79.4 s [O] |
| Key contrast | "not X, but Y" | Show both |

**Words that do not trigger change:** restatements, qualifiers and sentences explaining what is already on screen.

**Proposed timing:** start the drawing 0 to 0.2 s after the word starts. This is a design choice. The research could not determine an exact lag.

## 13. Camera movement and transitions

**Observed:** Sam O'Nella and Casually Explained mostly use cuts and little camera movement. After Skool moves along its long board. TED-Ed uses soft transitions between painted scenes. Kurzgesagt uses graphic wipes. OverSimplified moves across maps.

**Proposed**

- Default camera: still.
- Slow push-in only on a hold.
- Quick zoom only for a punchline or detail.
- Slide along the board when the idea continues.
- Hard cut for a new place or a joke.
- Wipe the board for a major section change.
- Never move the camera during reading-heavy labels.

## 15. Measurable production rules [P]

| ID | Rule | Check |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | First frame is never empty | Frame 0 has a drawing |
| R2 | Every drawing links to a narration word | Each event has `anchor_word` |
| R3 | Drawing starts 0–0.2 s after its anchor word | Timing check |
| R4 | No whole-scene hold over 10 s without an added element | Timeline check |
| R5 | At least one hold beat of 6–10 s per 60 s | Beat count |
| R6 | At most 5 meaningful objects at once | Object count |
| R7 | At most one major gesture per sentence | Gesture count |
| R8 | One expression per beat | Expression count |
| R9 | Each reaction follows its anchor word | Timing check |
| R10 | The ending reuses an opening object | Object ID check |
| R11 | Labels have at most 3 words | Text check |
| R12 | No gag beats in serious-topic scenes | Tone flag check |

These starting values should be tuned after a side-by-side test.

## 16. Future bot implementation requirements

This describes future work. Do not build it during the research phase.

**Script input:** each line carries `SHOW`, `HOLD` or `TURN`, plus an optional `tone` of `normal`, `comic` or `serious`.

**Beat plan output**

```json
{
  "beat_type": "build",
  "start": 12.4,
  "end": 14.6,
  "anchor_word": "gauge",
  "board_action": "add",
  "objects": [{"id": "gauge1", "state": "low", "reuse_of": null}],
  "tory": {"pose": "point", "target": "gauge1", "expression": "curious"},
  "camera": "still",
  "transition": "none"
}
```

**Required abilities**

1. Word-level timing from the narration audio.
2. Keeping objects across beats, so the board can grow.
3. Changing an object's state without redrawing it.
4. A pose and expression vocabulary for Tory.
5. Tone flags that turn comedy off.
6. A validator for the rules in section 15.
7. A callback tracker for opening and ending objects.

**Test plan:** render one script the current way and one with these rules, then compare them by eye and by retention data if available.

## 17. Reference videos, timestamps and research evidence

### Method

- **Sample:** for each of 12 channels, the 3 most-viewed videos of 90–1,200 seconds among the latest 80 uploads listed: 36 videos.
- **Downloaded:** public videos at up to 360p with yt-dlp, kept in a temporary folder for analysis. YouTube's terms may restrict downloading, so Venloud should decide if this method is acceptable for future bot use.
- **Visual events:** first 5 minutes at 5 frames per second, reduced to 96×54 pixels. A change frame has over 1.2% of pixels changed. Peak under 10% is **small**, 10–35% is **big**, and over 35% is **cut**.
- **Speech:** YouTube caption word timings. No audio signal was analyzed.
- **Frames viewed:** 12 of the 36 videos. Four priority videos got one frame per second from 60–90 s. Eight others got one frame per 2.4 s over the first 72 s.

### Limits

- **Not measured:** blinks, reaction delay, zoom timing and character motion. These need full-frame-rate work on selected scenes.
- **Change counts are approximate.** Pose changes, hand movement, draw-ons and transitions can all count as events. TheOdd1sOut's number likely includes pose changes, and After Skool's includes its hand.
- **Only opening sections were studied,** so endings and long-range callbacks are thinly supported.
- **Sponsor segments are included** in some first-five-minute windows.
- **The sample is small and popularity-biased,** and everything is widescreen while The Lab is vertical.
- **Captions vary in quality.** Two videos had too few timed words to use for speech rates.
- **Sisyphus 55's sampled titles include very heavy subjects.** That tone is not a Lab model.
- **The reference videos are for study only.** No character, art, script or identity should be copied.
