BOURIKO: There is a small man inside every traffic light. He decides who goes. He does not like me.
ROCK PHONE: There is no man. Look down.
BOURIKO: The road?
ROCK PHONE: Under many intersections is a loop of wire cut into the pavement. Electricity runs through it and makes a magnetic field.
ROCK PHONE: When a car stops on top, the metal changes that field. A box on the corner notices the change and tells the light someone is waiting.
BOURIKO: So the road can feel me.
ROCK PHONE: In a way. Other intersections use cameras or radar instead. And some lights ignore you completely and just run on a timer.
BOURIKO: Then why does my friend on his bicycle wait forever?
ROCK PHONE: Less metal. A bicycle can be too small for the loop to notice. Some roads paint a mark showing where a bike should stop.
BOURIKO: So I must bring more metal.
ROCK PHONE: Please do not bring more metal.
