NARRATOR: How does a traffic light actually know you're there?
NARRATOR: A lot of intersections use sensors buried in the road.
NARRATOR: Look at the pavement near the stop line. You may see a rectangle cut into the road.
NARRATOR: That can be an induction loop: wire buried under the pavement with electricity running through it.
NARRATOR: When a car stops over the loop, the metal changes the loop's magnetic field.
NARRATOR: A controller detects that change and tells the traffic signal a vehicle is waiting.
NARRATOR: But not every light uses a loop.
NARRATOR: Some intersections use cameras or radar. Others simply follow a timer or a programmed schedule.
NARRATOR: And bicycles can be tricky for induction loops because they contain much less metal than a car.
NARRATOR: Some intersections have bicycle detection areas or use other sensors instead.
NARRATOR: So next time you stop at a light, look at the pavement.
NARRATOR: If you see those rectangular cuts near the stop line, you may be looking at the sensor that is telling the light you're there.
NARRATOR: And those cameras mounted above the signal? That's a whole different system.
