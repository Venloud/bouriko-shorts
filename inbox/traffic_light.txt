NARRATOR: How does a traffic light know your car is waiting?
NARRATOR: It can actually feel your car sitting on the road.
NARRATOR: Many roads have a hidden sensor under the pavement.
NARRATOR: Look near the white stop line. You might see a rectangle cut into the road.
NARRATOR: That rectangle can be a wire loop hiding under the pavement.
NARRATOR: Electricity runs through the wire, making an invisible magnetic field.
NARRATOR: When a car stops on top of it, the car changes that field.
NARRATOR: The sensor notices the change and tells the traffic light, "Hey, a car is here."
NARRATOR: Then the light can decide when it is time to let that car go.
NARRATOR: But some traffic lights use cameras or radar instead.
NARRATOR: And some simply change because a timer says it is time.
NARRATOR: So next time you stop at a red light, look down at the road.
NARRATOR: That little rectangle might be the hidden sensor that knows you are there.
NARRATOR: The traffic light is not guessing. It is getting a signal from the road.
